Portifo · Design System · Proposal

Transactions — redesign

Three complaints and one open decision, answered together. The screen cannot tell a Buy from a Sell at a glance; a row states less about a transaction than the transaction has to state; and the filter row is three controls crushed into 113pt each. screens.html already flags the last one — “either the filters get designed into this card or they get dropped — open decision, and until it is made the shipped screen has one element with no spec.” This page closes it. Option B is chosen (section 03) and section 05 follows from that choice: B drops the glyph, so the type chip becomes the only cue for what kind of movement a row is, and it earns colour — S3′, one hue per type, tuned so the four survive colour-vision deficiency. Nothing is implemented yet — per rule 14 the approved version moves into guidelines.html / screens.html / foundations/ first, and only then into packages/portifo-web.

01 — What is actually wrong

Not a matter of taste — each of the three is a specific decision that can be named and reversed.

SymptomCauseMove
Buy vs Sell vs Deposit vs Withdraw don't separate Four types share two glyphs. Buy and Deposit are the same arrow; Sell and Withdraw are the same arrow. The only thing separating them is the well tint, and the only thing naming them is the word Buy set in 12px body face, --fg-2, glued to the account inside the subtitle — the quietest string in the row. Four glyphs on a 2 × 2 grid (direction × what moved), and the type promoted out of the subtitle into a chip on the ticker's line.
A row says too little Shares and price were deliberately cut — “share maths stays on the detail screen — at these name lengths it collided with the account.” True at the time: both were fighting for the same subtitle. But the right column's second line was spending itself on Jul 12, a six-character figure. Swap them. The date joins the account on the subtitle; 12 sh @ $187.32 takes the right column, directly under the total it multiplies to.
The filter row is cramped flex:1 on three chips splits 353pt into 113pt each, and each chip then spends a 10.5pt uppercase label on top of its value. Wealthsimple TFSA arrives as Wealthsim… in a control whose whole job is to show you what you filtered by. Content-sized chips on one line. The chip shows its dimension when unset and its value when set — the label was saying what the value already says.
02 — Telling the four types apart

Three cues, no hue. --gain/--loss stay the return system — a Buy is not a loss — and --signal stays spent on the header action and the tab marker. So differentiation has to come from shape, from the word, and from the well the app already uses to separate cash from trades.

Cue 1 · the glyph, on a 2 × 2 griddirection × what moved
Buy
Arrow onto a bare line. A position arrives.
Sell
Arrow off the line. A position leaves.
Deposit
Arrow into an open tray. Cash arrives.
Withdraw
Arrow out of the tray. Cash leaves.
The arrow axis is unchanged — it still reads direction, and it is still never tinted --gain/--loss. What is new is the second axis: a bare line for a trade, an open tray for a cash movement. Today that axis exists only as the well tint, which is a 10%-alpha difference and the first thing to go under a bright screen. The tint stays as well — it is what matches the Cash row on Portfolio — so trade vs cash now carries two cues and direction carries one.
Cue 2 · the type is a chip, not prose
AAPL Buy NVDA Sell USD Deposit EUR Withdraw
Same box as .w-tag and .closed-tag: 9.5px mono, uppercase, 1px --border, no fill, --fg-3. One chip vocabulary — a quiet caption stating one fact about the row, at a weight that cannot compete with the money on the right. The type moves from the quietest string in the row to the primary line, without gaining a single point of size or a drop of colour.
Considered and cut
Superseded

Colour the type. Written here as a “don't”, and reversed in section 05 once Option B was chosen: B deletes the glyph, so the chip stops being one of three cues and becomes the only one. Green Buy / red Sell stays wrong — a Buy is not a loss, and it puts a third meaning on the two hues reserved for return — but that rules out those hues, not colour.

Don't

Sign the total. −$2,247.84 for a Buy would differentiate instantly at the value column — and would be a lie about the ledger: a losing Sell still receives positive cash, and the sign would have to mean cash-flow direction while the glyph means portfolio direction. Two axes, one mark.

Don't

Put an arrow inside the chip. ↓ BUY restates the glyph three characters away from it. The row already says which way it went.

Option A · build notes
RowWhat moved · what happened · how much, and how it got there. .sym is the ticker (currency for cash) plus the type chip; the subtitle is date · account; .val is the total; .meta is the derivation, 12 sh @ $187.32, sitting directly under the number it multiplies to. That adjacency is the design — the row shows its own arithmetic, which is what a trade confirmation does.
The swapNothing was added to the row. The date left the right column for the subtitle and the share maths took its place. Share maths was cut once because it collided with the account on the same line — in different columns they never meet. Measured at 393pt: the account gets 111–145pt, enough for Fidelity Brokerage (102pt) and Wealthsimple TFSA (109pt) whole. The sell row is the tightest at 111pt — its P&L line is the widest thing in the right column — so an account past roughly 19 characters clips there first and nowhere else.
AlignmentThe row top-aligns. A cash movement has no derivation line, so a centred right column floated its total to the middle of the row; hanging from the top keeps .val on the ticker's baseline in all four types. The chevron re-centres itself.
PrecisionShares to at most 4 decimals, trailing zeros trimmed (0.7482 sh); price at the currency's own precision. The derivation line never wraps and never truncates — it is the widest thing in the column and the account absorbs the variation.
SellKeeps its realized-P&L third line, now under the derivation. Unlabelled: +$412.60 · +12.2%. Adding Realized: cost 50pt of the account's width to say what the only tinted figure in a ledger row could already only mean.
AmountsUnchanged — the transaction's own currency, unsigned, neutral ink. €1,200.00 stays euros.

Option B · build notes
RailThe glyph's 30pt becomes a day stamp. The month is already the section head, so the rail states only what varies inside the group; the weekday is what makes a bare 12 read as a date rather than a quantity. A column of descending days is what a statement looks like, and it makes the month grouping visible instead of merely stated.
What it buysThe subtitle carries the account alone, at full width — no truncation at any account name the app can produce. This is the only option where that is true.
What it costsThe glyph, and with it the direction cue. Type differentiation falls entirely to the chip word. That is defensible by this system's own logic — a glyph that says ↓ next to a chip that says BUY is the same fact twice, and the doc has already deleted two marks for exactly that — but it is one cue where A has three, against a complaint that is specifically about telling types apart.
Also costsEvery other list in the app leads with a glyph or a ticker. This list would lead with neither, which is a second row vocabulary for one screen.
Identical to AFilter row, glyph-free leading slot aside, right column, chip, month grouping and sell line are the same. The leading 30pt is the only variable — judge the two on that alone.
04 — Filters, and the open decision

screens.html says the filter row is either designed in or dropped. It should be designed in — a ledger without a filter is a scroll — but it should stop costing 54pt of every fold to say All three times. Two ways to do that; they are compatible with either row.

F1 · content-sized chips — recommended34pt, one line, scrolls
Symbol Type Account
AAPL Buy Account Clear
Symbol Wealthsimple TFSA Clear
Unset, partly set, and set with a long account name. Measured slack to the far gutter: 90pt, 48pt, 30pt — Wealthsimple TFSA plus a Clear still clears it, and anything longer scrolls rather than truncating. Each chip still opens the same PickerSheet; choosing All clears it, and Clear resets all three at once and exists only while something is set. No new colour: on is --surface-2 + --fg-1, off is transparent + --fg-3, which is exactly how .seg-item.active already separates on from off.

Corrected after build: the rows above put Clear inside the scroller, and that is wrong — with Symbol and Account both set it was pushed 41px past a 390pt viewport, hiding the reset exactly when the most filters were on. Shipped structure is .f-row > .f-scroll (dimensions only, with a 16px trailing mask) and .f-clear as a sibling outside it. See the Filter row entry in guidelines.html, which is the spec.

05 — Colour on the type chip

Section 02 argued the chip should stay monochrome. Option B changes that argument, and it should be said plainly rather than left contradicting itself: B deletes the glyph, so the chip is no longer one of three cues, it is the only cue for what kind of movement a row is. A single 9.5px grey word carrying the whole type axis is thin. Colour is the right answer here — but only a colour system that survives measurement, and only one that leaves --gain/--loss and --signal meaning what they already mean. Four hues get there, but not the four you would pick by eye.

Four hues: not by eye, but yes when tunedCIEDE2000, Viénot–Brettel–Mollon

The first two rows are why four hues look like a trap: picked by eye they collapse, because red and green are both spoken for and those are exactly the poles a four-way set wants, so the remaining hues crowd. Worst measured collision was ΔE 0.6 — invisible. But hue was never the only axis. Under dichromacy colour is roughly two-dimensional: one chromatic axis plus lightness, and lightness is untouched. Spreading the same four hue identities across L*31–50 instead of bunching them at L*46–55 lifts the tightest pair from ΔE 2.4 to 15.2, about double the ΔE 6.5 the guidelines already call too close. Four hues work — they just cannot all sit at the same value.

06 — Recommendation

Option B + S3′ + F1. The day rail, one colour per transaction type, and content-sized filter chips.

S3 was chosen for the reason it is attractive — four types, four colours, nothing to decode — and the objection to it turned out to be an objection to how it was drawn, not to four hues as such. Four hues bunched at one lightness collapse; four hues spread across L*31–50 do not. S3′ keeps the palette that was picked and moves only what had to move.

S1 and S2 stay documented as the fallbacks. If the four badges read as too busy down a long list on device — four tinted chips per screenful is materially more colour than this app has carried anywhere — S2 is the retreat: same row, same tokens, two hues instead of four.

To implement: --tx-buy / --tx-sell / --tx-deposit / --tx-withdraw and their tints go into foundations/tokens.css — all three theme blocks, per rule 05 — with a note recording that this is the second categorical set after --cat-1..4, that it is keyed to a fixed set of four whose meaning never changes (which is exactly what --cat-* lacked, being keyed by rank), and that the lightness spread is load-bearing, not styling: flattening it re-breaks the palette. The day rail, .tx-tag and its four variants, the row's top-alignment and the derivation line go into guidelines.html (components → a Transaction row entry beside Holding row) and foundations/base.css; the filter chips get a Filter row entry; the Not drawn spec row on the Transactions card in screens.html is deleted and its card redrawn from Option B. Only then does TransactionsPage.tsx / TransactionRow.tsx change — rule 14. Rule 12 still owes a check on device: these hues have been measured, not yet seen on an iPhone in sunlight.
