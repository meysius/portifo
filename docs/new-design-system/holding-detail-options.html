The real problem is the split, not the density

Your own data makes the case. NVDA is held in 5 accounts across 7 open lots. To read those 7 lots today you push into an account, read, come back, push into the next — 10 navigations, holding numbers in your head the whole way, because no screen ever shows two accounts' lots at once.

And the thing that split hides is not hypothetical. Sorted by date, your two largest lots — 268 sh in Meysam's TFSA and 173 sh in Haniyeh's TFSA — were bought on the same day, at $221.27 and $221.74, and are both about 6% underwater. They are the two biggest bets in the position and they are the same bet. Today they live on different screens, two pushes apart, and nothing in the app will ever put them side by side.

That is worth fixing before any typography. So: yes — merge. The argument is already written in Account Holding's own spec, which describes it as "the same block as the parent page at a smaller magnification" carrying "NO security-level content". A screen that is its parent at a smaller magnification is not a second view of the data — it is a filter. It adds a navigation layer and no new information, and the price of that filter grows with every account you open.

01
Nothing is lost by flattening except the per-account rollup and two scoped buttons, and the rollup is a five-row table — it does not need a screen each.
02
The screen gets long, and that is correct. 7 lots and 5 accounts runs to roughly 1.5 screens. Scrolling one list beats 10 navigations, and it is the only arrangement in which cross-account comparison is possible at all.
03
The bars are gone. They asked the reader to learn an encoding to read a list — the wrong trade on a screen whose complaint was that it is hard to read. What survives from the last pass is the part that needed no explaining: rank the figures, tint only percentages, give the returns a real column.

Notes on the two alternatives

Alt 1 · Ledgerdensest545pt

The lot stops being a list item and becomes a table row: five labelled columns, no connective words. 28pt a lot against the rail's 61pt.

01
Every joining word is gone. at, the dots, the spelled-out date — all of them existed only to relate two figures, and a column header does that job once instead of on every row. This is rule 10 followed to its conclusion: these are comparable figures, so they belong in columns with a header, not in a sentence.
02
It gives up the exact purchase date, and that was forced. Five columns leave 341pt of content. A 2-digit date measures 58pt and a 4-digit price 65pt; my first pass at 53/54pt clipped the date immediately and would clip the price the day NVDA passes $1,000. Swapping BOUGHT for AGE costs 13pt in a 34pt column and brings the grid to 312pt with 29pt of slack. The date still lives on the transaction that created the lot — but it is off this screen, and the other two options keep it.
03
The header appears twice, not five times. Only multi-lot accounts get one; single-lot accounts collapse to their head as before. A holding spread over eight accounts with two lots each would carry eight headers, which is the condition under which this option gets worse rather than better.
04
Value and return still align with the head. Verified: both right edges land at 324pt and 394pt in the head and in every ledger row. The head keeps its own 1fr 98 60 grid and the ledger has a different one, so only the right edges are shared — change either template and re-measure, because the alignment is doing the work here.
Alt 2 · Cardsclearest grouping614pt

Containment by box instead of by rail. Five discrete objects, so an account's extent is unambiguous rather than inferred from a rule plus a rail. Idiomatic — the app's accounts and lots lists are already IonList inset.

01
The head sits on --surface-2, which makes the head/lot distinction structural instead of only typographic. It is the one thing the rail version leans entirely on weight and face to convey.
02
A single-lot card carries no tint (.solo). Tinted, three of the five cards read as a header waiting for content that never arrives — a card with nothing under it is a row, not a head. This was visible only once rendered.
03
It is the tallest, and the boxes are the reason. 614pt against the rail's 573 and the ledger's 545 — five borders, five radii and four 10pt gaps. It also introduces the most new chrome to a design system whose light mode is deliberately "a single white plane where a card is separated by a 1px hairline and nothing else". Five tinted strips is the furthest of the three from that.

Build notes

01
The lot row: two lines, three columns. Line 1 is identity and verdict — account name, market value, return. Line 2 is the receipt — 160 at $13.55 · Mar 15, 2021 · 5y 5m on the left, the dollar delta right-aligned. Line 2 spans the first two grid columns so the receipt gets its width while the 64pt return column stays clear.
02
The account name leads, in the display face. This inverts the old row, where 60 × $13.20 was the name. In a list that spans accounts, which account is what you scan for, and an account name is a proper noun — the same reasoning .row-name already applies on the Accounts section. The purchase figures drop to line 2 in --fg-3: a price paid in 2021 is a caption on a past event, not a peer of the market value beside it. Rule 10 is intact — the figures stay mono; only which of them is allowed to be --fg-1 changed.
03
Sorted oldest first, across accounts. Same sort the section already used, now spanning the whole holding — which is what makes the two same-day May 18 lots land next to each other. This is the entire point of the merge.
04
Accounts becomes a 40pt one-line rollup. A two-line 61pt row with a chevron was the shape of a destination; once it pushes nowhere (rule 09 — the chevrons must come off) it should read as a table: name, shares, value, return. Five rows fit in 200pt instead of 305pt. Dropped from the old per-account block: per-account cost basis and avg age. Per-account avg cost is dropped from the row too — every lot beneath it carries its own price, which is the better version of that fact. Say if any of those three is load-bearing and it goes back as a second line.
05
Scoped Buy/Sell are lost, and that is the real cost of the merge. Today Account Holding opens Add Transaction with the account and symbol prefilled. Merged, the page-level buttons can only prefill the symbol and the account gets picked in Add Transaction — which already has an account field. Arguably better: you no longer navigate into an account in order to pre-select it. But it is a real regression for the two-tap buy, so it is the thing to weigh.
06
Long account names. Longest in the data is Fidelity Brokerage at 18 characters. Both the rollup name and the lot's account name ellipsize rather than wrap — a wrapped name would break the row into three lines and pull the meta line out of alignment with the return column beside it.
07
Grouped by account — and why the first mock of it was unreadable. Four separate failures, all fixed in the rebuild: (a) the head's figures were pushed out with margin-left:auto, so an account's total landed at an arbitrary x-position instead of above the lot values that sum to it — the alignment is the reason to group, and it was the one thing missing. (b) The hierarchy was inverted: the group head was 13px/600 --fg-2 while its own lots were 14px/600 --fg-1, so the child outranked the parent. (c) Every row carried a hairline, so five groups read as one long list with occasional bold lines; now only accounts are separated by a rule and an account's lots are bound together by a rail instead. (d) The lot's purchase figures were sitting in .lot-acct, which sets the display face — a mono figure in the display face, straight against rule 10.
08
Grouping deletes the Accounts table, which is the real argument for it. The group heads are the rollup, so the screen goes from two sections that restate each other to one section that states each fact once — the exact duplication flagged below as the flat layout's weak point. Measured: the grouped section is 573pt against 677pt for a separate Accounts table plus a flat lot list. Shorter and non-repeating.
09
An account with one lot renders as one row. Three of your five NVDA accounts hold a single lot, where the head and the lot are the same object — so there is no group to draw, and the head's meta line carries the lot's receipt (160 at $13.55 · Mar 15, 2021 · 5y 5m) instead of a shares/avg-cost summary. This is not a layout that changes shape by count; it is a group of one having no grouping to show. Without it, half the screen is a head followed by one child repeating its numbers.
10
What grouping costs. The cross-account chronology goes. Your two same-day May 18 lots land in different groups again, and the ages restart in every group — so the insight that made the strongest case for merging is not visible in this arrangement. It is still one screen and zero navigations, which was the complaint; but if you want both readings, the honest way is a By account / By date segmented control over the one section, since the two views are the same rows in a different order.
11
A single-account holding. Most of your holdings are in one account. There the Accounts section is one row and the lot rows all repeat the same name — noise. Options: keep it (one layout, no special cases, consistent with the single-account rule already in the spec), or drop the lot row's account line when the holding has one account. Recommend keeping it; a one-row table is cheap and a layout that changes shape by account count is the thing rule 03 warns about. Flagging it because it is a judgement call.
12
Closed positions. AssetDetailPage already inverts for a closed holding — realized takes the hero, Market and the open stats go, accounts lose their chevron. That path is unaffected by the merge except that the chevron removal is now unconditional, which simplifies it: no agg.closed branch on the accounts rows at all.
13
What gets deleted. AccountHoldingPage.tsx, its route /asset/:symbol/account/:account, and the Account Holding device card in screens.html. The lots move into AssetDetailPage, flattened from agg.perAccount[].lots[] with the account name carried onto each lot. No API or data-model change — positions.ts already computes everything this screen needs.

Which arrangement

Both are one screen and zero navigations, so the merge is settled either way. What is left is the order of the rows, and it is a genuine trade rather than a better/worse:

A
Grouped by account — one ACCOUNTS section, group heads that are the rollup, lots bracketed underneath. States every fact once, deletes the separate Accounts table, 573pt, and an account's total sits in the same column as the lots that sum to it. Answers "what is in each account" perfectly. Cannot answer "what are my oldest / most underwater lots" — the chronology is broken across groups.
B
Flat, oldest first — a rollup table plus one chronological list spanning accounts, 677pt. Answers "what are all my lots" perfectly, which is where the two same-day underwater May 18 lots become visible. Costs the duplication: three of five accounts have one lot, so three rollup rows repeat a lot row exactly.
A+
Grouped, with a By account / By date segment — same rows, two orders, one section. It is the only arrangement that answers both questions, and the segment is honest about being a sort rather than a new view. Costs one control on a screen whose complaint was that it is hard to read, so it should be added only if you find yourself wanting the flat order once the grouped one is live.

I would ship A and see whether you miss the chronology before building the segment. A is the better structure — it is the version where nothing is stated twice — and the cross-account chronology, though it produced the sharpest insight in your data, is the rarer question.

On the three lot treatments, all grouped, all measured: Rail 573pt · Ledger 545pt · Cards 614pt. Ledger is the most scannable and the only one that loses the purchase date. Cards group most unambiguously and add the most chrome. Rail sits between them and adds nothing to the design system — it is one absolutely-positioned 2px rule. If none of the three is obviously right, that is a signal to ship Rail, which is the cheapest to change later.

Open questions

Three things I would not decide for you:

?
Is the two-tap scoped buy worth keeping a screen for? If yes, the merge still works — Account Holding survives as a rarely-visited leaf, but the Accounts rows keep their chevrons and the lots appear in both places, which is duplication I would rather avoid.
?
Does the Accounts rollup still earn its place? The render says: only half. Three of your five NVDA accounts hold exactly one lot — so Fidelity Brokerage · 160 sh · $33,336.00 · +1438% appears in the rollup and then again, number for number, as a lot row 200pt further down. The rollup only adds information for the two accounts that hold more than one lot (Meysam's TFSA, Haniyeh's TFSA). Options: keep it as-is (one shape, no special cases), cut it entirely and let the lots carry the accounts, or show a rollup row only where an account has 2+ lots — which is honest but makes the table's row count stop matching the section's 5. I lean keep, because the duplication is cheap and predictable while a conditional table is neither, but this is the weakest part of the screen and worth your call.
?
Is oldest-first still right when the list spans accounts? An alternative is largest-first, which would put your two underwater $36k–56k lots at the top instead of the 2021 lot that can only ever look good. Oldest-first is what the section already did; largest-first is arguably the better default now that the list is the screen's centre of gravity.
