The key is unlearnable, and the legend is the holdings list printed twice

Everything below is drawn with the real contents of My Portfolio at today's quotes, because the design fails specifically at this portfolio's size. Seven slices — cash plus six positions — and the shipped code has CAT_COLORS[i % 4]. NVDA at 47.3% and URA at 5.9% render the same blue. QQQ at 9.5% and IBIT at 3.2% render the same gold. The mapping isn't hard, it's ambiguous.

Why it can't be fixed by making the dot bigger
01
The key is assigned by rank, so it cannot be learned. CAT_COLORS[i] keys off position in the value-sorted list. QQQ and SMH are $21,837 and $21,681 apart by 0.7% — one ordinary day swaps them, and gold becomes purple. A key that changes when prices move is not a key, it's a coincidence the reader is asked to re-derive on every visit.
02
With this portfolio it isn't even unique. i % 4 recycles at the fifth holding: NVDA/URA share --cat-1, QQQ/IBIT share --cat-2. The guidelines say the tail should aggregate into "Other" past --cat-4 — the code recycles instead. Either way six positions is normal and four hues is the ceiling.
03
--cat-1 aliases --signal. The largest slice is painted in the one accent, which the colour budget reserves for primary buttons, back links, the active tab marker and focus rings. The biggest holding is therefore the same blue as every interactive thing on the screen.
04
The legend is a second holdings list. Same items, same order, and less in each one: a label and a percentage where the row below has value, today and total. Seven chips exist to explain an encoding, then a divider, then the seven rows they explain. The section boundary is the mapping problem — the reader must carry a key across it.
05
The chart restates the hero at 130pt. The hero already gives Today (−$6,266.77 · −3.14%) and Total, each in money and percent. The card adds shape, and a seven-target range row — the densest control on the screen — to a question already answered 40pt above. It is also the only block fed by its own endpoint, so it is the screen's most fragile 130pt: spinner, "not enough data", or a curve. At rest it pushes the list down to two visible rows out of seven.
A · Weight, not colourrecommendeddeletes the Allocation section

The mapping problem is solved by removing the thing being mapped. The bar, the legend and the category palette all go; each row states its own weight, in the 34pt slot the colour dot was occupying. The list is already sorted by value, so the leading column reads 47 · 16 · 10 · 9 · 9 · 6 · 3 — a descending column of real numbers that needs no key, survives forty holdings, and can't collide with itself.

The one thing the stacked bar did well was make concentration a shape you saw rather than a number you read. That comes back for almost nothing: each row's separator carries a 2pt underline as long as the row's weight. No new colour, no new section — and because the rows descend, the underlines form the bar chart the stacked bar was trying to be, rotated so that every bar sits on the same line as its own label.

The first attempt drew that bar inside the 1px hairline — filled to the weight, --border for the remainder. Rendered, it read as a broken separator rather than as data, which is why the bar now sits on top of an intact hairline instead. Both renders are below; the rejected one is worth knowing about before someone tries it again.

What B still costs
01
Rank is still the key. Fixing uniqueness does not fix instability — QQQ and SMH are 0.7% apart, and the day they swap, two rows and two slices change colour with no event the reader can point to.
02
In-slice text needs a second ink rule. White reads on --cat-1/3/4, is marginal on --cat-2's #B8862E, and fails on --cat-cash's #9AA0A6 — so the bar needs a per-hue ink switch that exists nowhere else in the language.
03
URA and IBIT lose their identity. Aggregating the tail is the honest fix for four hues, but the two rows in "+2" then have a grey rail keying them to a segment with no name. Nine holdings makes that seven rows.
04
It still spends a section and 84pt on a fact A states in the rows for free — and it keeps the reader crossing a divider with a key in their head, which is the original complaint.

Worth building only if the stacked bar is something you actively like looking at. It is a real improvement on today; it just does not remove the class of problem.

The chart: keep the curve, stop giving it a card

There is one thing a value line does that nothing else on this screen does — it shows shape: whether $229,589 is a plateau, a climb or a recovery. That is worth about 34pt. What it is not worth is a bordered card, a 7-target range row, a spinner state and an empty state, all to re-answer the question the hero answers in words directly above it.

Movers had exactly one objection against it: every figure in the block also sat in the rows' Today: line. That resolves in the block's favour — the line moves out of the rows and into the block, where it is ranked, scaled and given a bar. Every row loses a line, which is most of what the block costs.

The weight column stays as it is in A2 — the figure in ink, the 2pt bar in --fg-3, no colour. A sequential ramp on the tiles was drawn and dropped; the record is in the last section. The screen therefore carries no categorical colour at all, which is the simplest possible answer to the diagnosis: nothing to key, nothing to learn.

Decisions inside this section
01
Today leaves the rows. It is not deleted — it is promoted. The block carries the same money and the same percentage, plus a rank and a scale the rows could never show. Rows keep value and Total, and drop from 72pt to a uniform 53–55pt.
02
The block replaces the chart rather than joining it. Both answer "what happened"; only one of them answers "because of what". Long-run shape moves to a pushed Performance screen off the hero.
03
Diverging, not ranked left-to-right. A centre baseline spends half the width, and on a one-way day like today one arm sits empty — that is the trade for sign that survives colour blindness and needs no legend. Left-anchored bars buy back ~90pt of bar length if the block ever feels cramped.
04
Long lists truncate, they do not scroll. Past six movers the block shows the top five and a +12 more · −$412 tail line, so the section has a fixed ceiling of about 130pt.
05
Cash has no row here. It does not move, and a permanent neutral tick at the baseline would be six pixels of noise on every render.
06
The hero drops its Today line. Rendered side by side, the hero said "Today" 40pt above a section head that also said "Today". The day's total moves to that head as its .dm meta — the one place in the system where a divider's meta is tinted, because it is money — and the hero keeps Total alone. Nothing is lost and the hero is a line shorter.
07
Nothing on this screen is coloured except money. With --cat-* retired and no ramp replacing it, the palette in play is --gain/--loss for figures and --signal for the two interactive marks. One pair, one accent — and weight is carried entirely by a figure and a bar length.

Build notes

01
The weight bar rides over the hairline, it does not replace it. The row keeps its border-bottom; ::after adds height:2px; width:var(--w); background:var(--fg-3) at bottom:-1px. That is not the rule-01 trap — the hairline is --border and the bar is --fg-3, so nothing paints --border twice. --w is the only per-row inline value.
02
Ionic detail. IonItem draws its separator inset via ::part(native), so a bar anchored to the item's own box will start 16px left of where that line begins. Set lines="none" and own both the hairline and the bar on one wrapper. Measure it (rule 11) before calling it done.
03
The weight column is 30pt wide with a 20pt gutter, and the ticker lands at 72pt. The width is set by the widest figure the cell must hold — 100% at 13px mono is 29pt — and everything left over goes to the gutter. The first cut had it backwards at 34 + 12, which left 13pt of dead space against the screen edge and put two mono figures 12pt apart. Same 50pt, spent the other way.
04
The name line clips, it does not wrap. white-space:nowrap + text-overflow:ellipsis. Row height was a function of how verbose Yahoo's shortName is — "VanEck Semiconductor ETF" ran to three lines and cost a row off the fold. Uniform rows are worth more than the tail of a name the ticker already identifies. Scoped to the holdings list, not to .name everywhere — check Accounts before generalising it.
05
Weights are integers, and they must sum to 100. Round half-up and give the largest holding the remainder, or the column reads 99 or 101. Anything under 0.5% shows <1% rather than 0%.
06
--cat-* becomes dead on this screen. Under A the palette has no remaining call site, and --cat-1's aliasing of --signal stops being a problem by not existing. Delete the "Category colors" block from guidelines.html in the same pass, or it documents a system nothing uses.
07
The glyph column goes with it. Cash keeps no icon — it is identified by the word "Cash", its currency sub-line and its weight, like every other row. That removes the second thing the current screen asks the eye to decode.
08
Sparkline range. One fixed range, fetched once. 1Y if there is a year of history, else All; the label states which. The PriceChart component already draws this — it needs a compact variant with no card, no axis and no RangePicker.
09
Closed positions are untouched. They keep their divider, their dimmed rows and realized P&L, and they get no weight cell — they have no weight.

Open questions

01
Do the weighted separators survive device? They are 1px of --fg-2 on a 353pt row. On the doc they read as intent; on glass at 3× they may read as an artifact, in which case A1 ships and nothing else changes.
02
Is weight worth the leading column, or should it sit under the value? Leading gives it a sorted column of its own, which is the whole argument. Trailing keeps the money together but buries it in a stack of three figures. Leading is the recommendation; it is a 20-minute change to test the other.
03
Answered by building it: six. The concern was that movers would cost too much of the fold. In the app the whole portfolio bar one row clears the tab bar, against two rows today. If it ever gets tight, the lever is the movers block's row gap and label width, not the list.
04
Does the long-run curve survive at all? Movers replaces the chart, so the value line has nowhere left on this screen. Either it moves to a pushed Performance screen off the hero, or it goes. Worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering later that it quietly vanished.
05
Cash inside the weights, or beside them? Cash at 16% is the second-largest line in this portfolio, so it belongs in the ranking. But it is also the one row with no return, which makes it the odd row in a list where every other row has two P&L lines. Shown in-list here.

Considered and dropped

—
A sequential colour ramp on the weight column. Four steps of one hue keyed to weight bands, tile fills with the figure in ink on top. It was technically sound — colour as a function of the value rather than the rank, so it fixed the instability that broke the category palette, and it validated (monotone OKLab lightness both modes, text 6.3–14.3:1). Dropped on preference: the screen does not want another colour system, and a figure plus a bar length already carries weight without one. Recorded so the idea is not re-proposed as new.
—
The weight bar drawn inside the hairline — see the rejected crop in section A. Read as a broken separator.
—
Option B, the self-labelling stacked bar. A real improvement on today, but it keeps a rank-assigned key, needs a per-hue ink switch for in-slice text, and still spends a section and 84pt on what the rows now state themselves.
